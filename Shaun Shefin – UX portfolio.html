MyFitnessPal: reducing cognitive overload

UX researcher and designer · Google UX Design Certificate project · 6 days

Problem

A cluttered interface, redundant steps and dense data displays lead to inconsistent logging habits.

Process

  • 6 interviews with gym-goers aged 18–25 who track macros daily
  • Zoom observations to map hesitation and friction points
  • Heuristic evaluation against Nielsen’s 10 usability principles

Findings

  • Deep dropdowns slow down logging standard meals
  • The macros screen shows too much text at once
  • Weak colour contrast and labels blur macro categories

Redesign

Progress bars replace heavy text lists, and streamlined day, week and month views cut extra clicks.

Concept screens

Concept screens illustrating the redesign decisions above, produced with AI assistance from my research findings. Sample data; not MyFitnessPal’s own interface.

Appliance use in rented homes

Lead UX researcher · mixed methods · survey N=14, interviews N=3

Problem

Renters inherit old appliances with confusing interfaces and missing manuals. Without bill incentives or trust in eco settings, they stick to one default cycle.

Process

  • Qualtrics survey for a quantitative baseline
  • Semi-structured interviews on routines and bottlenecks
  • Neutral, non-leading questions and anonymity to reduce bias

Insights

  • Eco trust gap: 54% prioritise cleaning results, 15% prioritise environmental savings
  • Bill structure: when bills are included in rent, the motivation to save energy disappears
  • Decision fatigue: faded dials and missing manuals push renters to one “safe” cycle

How might we…

  • make the eco experience feel like a reliable tool, not a marketing stunt?
  • help renters understand time, cost and efficiency without a manual?

The psychology of e-commerce design

Lead UX researcher and designer · undergraduate thesis

Problem

Minimalism reduces cognitive load, but luxury brands risk losing prestige if they look too plain.

Approach

  • 24 storefront mock-ups in Figma, varying background and typography
  • 2×2 within-subjects experiment in PsychoPy with 50 participants
  • Ratings and qualitative feedback, analysed with linear mixed-effects models in R

Findings

  • Sophisticated backgrounds raised perceived value and trust (p < .001)
  • Elegant typefaces created friction and hurt readability
  • Purchase intent peaked with a rich background and a simple typeface (p = .027)

Takeaway

Use rich visuals to build brand authority, and protect how easy the copy is to read.

Social media and anxiety

UX researcher · qualitative report

Methods

Semi-structured interviews and Braun and Clarke (2006) thematic analysis.

Core insight

Information overload and social comparison drive anxiety, so digital products need to minimise cognitive friction.

Cope Notes: UX and content evaluation

UX and content evaluator · text-based mental health service

Approach

  • Usability and accessibility audit of onboarding, engaging and pausing messages for users with anxiety
  • Content and tone analysis of how clinical CBT ideas are balanced with a human voice
  • Cognitive load review of message length and frequency

Findings

  • Empathetic, non-judgmental tone improves retention and emotional safety. In text products, words are the UI.
  • Plain SMS removes the barrier of downloading an app
  • Frequent or dense messages can add to notification fatigue

Takeaway

For stressed users, content should be concise and predictable. Accessibility includes cognitive and emotional accessibility.
